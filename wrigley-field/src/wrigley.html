      The history of Wrigley Field

      Ivy, lights, a goat, a called shot, and 108 years of waiting. Twelve moments from the corner of Clark and Addison.

      Weeghman Park rises

      Restaurant owner Charles Weeghman builds a steel-and-concrete ballpark at Clark and Addison, on the grounds of a former Lutheran seminary, for his Federal League club. Architect Zachary Taylor Davis designs it.

      $250KTo build
      ~7 weeksGround to opener
      14,000Seats

      The Cubs move in

      After the Federal League folds, Weeghman buys the Cubs and moves them to his park. On April 20, 1916, they beat the Reds 7–6 in 11 innings in their first game there.

      Fun factA live bear cub was on hand for the occasion.

      It becomes Wrigley Field

      Chewing-gum magnate William Wrigley Jr. now owns the Cubs, and the park takes his name. An upper deck goes up over the next two seasons.

      1. Weeghman Park1914–1919
      2. Cubs Park1920–1926
      3. Wrigley Field1926–today

      The Called Shot

      World Series, Game 3, fifth inning. Babe Ruth has two strikes on him, gestures toward center field, and hits the next pitch there.

      The legend

      Ruth pointed to the bleachers and called his home run before he hit it.

      The film

      Grainy footage shows a gesture, but nobody agrees if he was pointing at the bleachers, the Cubs dugout, or pitcher Charlie Root.

      Fun factCharlie Root, who threw the pitch, is in the 1929 team composite on the slide before this one.

      The ivy goes up

      Bill Veeck plants Boston ivy and bittersweet along the new outfield wall. The center-field scoreboard goes up the same year, and it's still turned by hand.

      Homer in the Gloamin'

      With darkness falling and the game about to be called, player-manager Gabby Hartnett homers in the bottom of the ninth to beat the Pirates. The Cubs take first place and go on to win the pennant.

      Fun fact"Gloaming" is an old word for dusk. Wrigley wouldn't get lights for another 50 years.

      The curse (allegedly)

      Billy Sianis, owner of the Billy Goat Tavern, brings his goat Murphy to Game 4 of the World Series. The goat is asked to leave. Legend says Sianis declared the Cubs would never win again. They lose the Series to Detroit.

      Mr. Cub hits 500

      On May 12, 1970, Ernie Banks hits his 500th career home run, off Atlanta's Pat Jarvis, at Wrigley.

      Harry takes the booth

      Harry Caray moves across town from the White Sox and leads Wrigley through "Take Me Out to the Ball Game" every seventh inning until 1997. After he dies in 1998, guest conductors keep the tradition going.

      "Holy cow!"

      8.8.88

      Let there be lights

      Wrigley was the last major league park without lights. The first night game started on 8/8/88, then got rained out in the fourth inning. The first official night game came the next night.

      100

      Happy birthday, Wrigley

      The park turns 100 on April 23, 2014. After the season, the 1060 Project renovation begins: rebuilt bleachers, video boards in 2015, and upgrades that run through 2019.

      November 2, 2016

      W

      Years since the Cubs last won the World Series

      The wait is over

      The Cubs win Game 7 in Cleveland, 8–7 in 10 innings, for their first title since 1908. Two nights later, the "W" flag flies over the marquee. On November 4, the parade starts at Clark and Addison.

      Fun factGames 3, 4 and 5 were the first World Series games at Wrigley since 1945.
